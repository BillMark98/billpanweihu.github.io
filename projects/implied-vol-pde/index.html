The problem

The implied volatility of a European call is defined implicitly: given an observed price Z, find the Sigma that solves BS(K, T, Sigma) = Z. The standard approach inverts this equation pointwise, once per quote, with a root solver.

Carr, Itkin and Stoikov (arXiv:1907.07305) do something different. They differentiate the defining identity, combine it with the Dupire forward equation, and obtain a nonlinear PDE that the implied volatility surface itself satisfies:

0 = Sigma_T - ½ Sigma² K² [ (Sigma_K/Sigma_Z)² Sigma_ZZ
                            - 2 (Sigma_K/Sigma_Z) Sigma_KZ + Sigma_KK ]
      + (r-q) K Sigma_K - q Z Sigma_Z

The PDE is model-free: it assumes only the Black–Scholes quoting convention, not that the underlying actually follows Black–Scholes.

This project implements two independent solvers for it, and benchmarks both against pointwise root-solving.

The singularity, and the change of variables that removes it

Sigma blows up as T → 0. Vega vanishes, the option collapses to its intrinsic value, and the implied volatility that reproduces a given price becomes arbitrarily sensitive. Any solver that marches forward in T from zero will start in exactly the worst-conditioned region of the domain.

The fix is to solve for total volatility instead:

Sbar(K,T,Z) = Sigma(K,T,Z) · √T

which satisfies the forward PDE with the clean initial condition Sbar(K,0,Z) = 0. Both solvers approximate Sbar and never touch Sigma directly. This one substitution is what makes the problem tractable.

The admissible domain is the no-arbitrage price band

(S e^{-qT} − K e^{-rT})⁺  <  Z  <  S e^{-qT}

a trapezoidal region in (K, T, Z), with Sbar = 0 on the lower intrinsic edge.

Solver 1 — finite differences

The paper observes that the forward PDE is really one-dimensional, with K along for the ride as a dummy variable. In hyperbolic form:

Sbar_T = −Θ(K,T,Sbar) · Sbar_Z + Sbar/(2T)

a transport equation in Z for each fixed K. I integrate it with forward Euler in T and first-order upwind differencing in Z — upwind because information flows toward larger Z, since Θ = ∂C/∂T > 0. Each output step is subdivided to satisfy the CFL condition, so the scheme stays stable for any requested number of time steps.

This is deliberately the simplified baseline, not the paper's full machinery (trapezoid-to-square transform, Strang splitting, implicit Crank–Nicolson). It runs on at-the-money and out-of-the-money strikes where the price band is well-conditioned. Deep in-the-money, intrinsic value varies too fast with maturity for a clean grid — a limitation I kept rather than papered over.

Solver 2 — a physics-informed neural network

An MLP maps (K,T,Z) → Sbar, trained on a composite objective:

L = w_pde·L_pde + w_ic·L_ic + w_bc·L_bc + w_data·L_data + w_arb·L_arb
  • L_pde — mean-squared PDE residual, derivatives via autograd
  • L_ic — the initial condition Sbar(K,0,Z) = 0
  • L_bc — Sbar = 0 on the lower price edge
  • L_data — supervised anchors from the root solver
  • L_arb — shape penalties enforcing Sbar_Z ≥ 0 and Sbar_K ≥ 0, which are structural no-arbitrage relations

Four design decisions mattered more than the architecture:

Softplus output, so positivity of Sbar is structural rather than learned.

Band-relative normalisation of Z. This is the one I would not have guessed in advance. A global scale for Z squashes every deep-OTM quote into a narrow sliver of the input range, and the network simply ignores them. Mapping Z to its position inside the volatility-bracketed price band gives those points room.

Epsilon-stabilising the division by Sbar_Z, which appears squared in the residual and is near zero exactly where the surface is flattest.

Generating training labels backwards. Rather than sampling (K,T,Z) and root-solving for the label, sample (K,T,sigma) directly, price Z = BS(K,T,sigma), and take Sbar = sigma√T as an exact label. No root-solving, no inversion error in the supervision signal.

Optimisation is Adam followed by L-BFGS.

Why build both

The point of two independent solvers is that they can disagree, and a disagreement is information. The root solver provides ground truth; the FD scheme and the PINN fail in different places for different reasons. experiments/compare.py overlays all three on a common price slice and reports the errors.

The test suite (49 passing, 2 skipped) covers the Black–Scholes primitives, the PDE residuals in all three forms — backward parabolic, forward parabolic, forward hyperbolic — and the no-arbitrage checks.

Layout

iv_pde/
  black_scholes.py    BS price, Vega, Theta, vectorised implied-vol solver
  domain.py           realistic vol-band sampling
  pde.py              forward parabolic residual
  pde_hyperbolic.py   forward hyperbolic residual
  pde_backward.py     backward parabolic residual
  no_arb.py           FD-neighbourhood arbitrage check + penalty
  fd_solver.py        finite-difference transport baseline
  pinn.py             MLP, band-normalised inputs, softplus output
  losses.py           pluggable PDE / IC / BC / data / arbitrage losses
  trainer.py          Adam + L-BFGS loop

Parameters live in config/default.yaml; notebooks/01_end_to_end.ipynb walks the whole pipeline.
