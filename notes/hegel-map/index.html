A map of Hegel's Phenomenology

An interactive structural map of the Phenomenology of Spirit — every shape of consciousness as a claim, the contradiction that breaks it, and the shape it is forced into next.

Status
In progress · §90–230 complete

On this page

  • What the map covers
  • The four moves
  • How to read alongside it

Reading the Phenomenology of Spirit straight through does not work. The book is not an argument with premises and a conclusion; it is a sequence of positions, each of which destroys itself and produces the next. Lose track of which position you are inside and the prose becomes unreadable — not difficult, unreadable.

So I drew a map. It has one row per shape of consciousness, and every row has the same four cells:

  • 自信 — what this shape is confident of. The claim it would make about where truth is.
  • 矛盾 / 崩溃 — the contradiction that shows up when the claim is taken seriously.
  • 退守与再败 — the retreat, and why the retreat fails too.
  • 被迫转向 — what it is forced to become.

The uniformity is the point. Hegel's chapters look wildly different from one another — sense-data, salt crystals, forces, a life-and-death struggle, Stoicism — and the identical structure underneath is exactly what is hard to hold onto while reading.

Drag to pan. ⌘ or ctrl with scroll to zoom. The map is in Chinese, with the German terms kept in the original; an English guide to its structure follows.

I would rather the map say where I have stopped than quietly imply I have read the whole thing.

The four moves

One panel on the map lists four terms that recur in every transition, and having them named is most of what makes the pattern visible:

否定 (Negation). Not "this is false." A determinate negation: the position fails in a specific way, and that specific way is the content of what comes next. This is why the book has an order.

中介 (Vermittlung). Nothing is simply immediate. Sense-certainty's whole claim is to grasp something directly, and the demonstration that even this is mediated is what the first chapter is for.

扬弃 (Aufhebung). Cancelled, preserved, and lifted, in one motion. The failed shape does not get discarded; it is carried into its successor as a moment.

承认 (Anerkennung). Arrives with self-consciousness and never leaves. A self is not a thing that then happens to encounter others — it is constituted in being recognised.

How to read alongside it

The instruction I wrote on the map to myself, in the box top-left:

Ask of each shape: what is it confident of? Where does it contradict itself? What is it forced to become?

And a second, about the Preface: understanding 30% is enough — go straight to the first chapter. The Preface was written last, after the whole book existed. It is a manifesto, not an introduction, and treating it as a gateway is the most common way to stall out on page four.

The third is about what the failures mean. Every shape on this map collapses. None of them is stupid — each is a position that serious people have held, stated in its strongest form. It is not Hegel refuting them from outside; it is the position discovering its own inadequacy from within. That distinction is the entire method.

On what this is. A reading aid for my own use, made while working through the text with commentaries. It is not scholarship and it is certainly not a substitute for the Phenomenology — which, as the map is meant to show, is precisely the kind of book a summary cannot replace.
